## Business Context

Operational teams depended on multiple platforms that each represented part of the customer and transaction lifecycle. Information moved between systems, but not always consistently, and that inconsistency created friction for execution and reporting alike.

## Problem

When integrations fail quietly, the pain often shows up as downstream confusion rather than obvious technical errors. Teams were spending time reconciling statuses, correcting mismatched fields, and debating which system should be trusted.

## My Role

I worked at the boundary between business process and technical delivery, tracing how records moved, where ownership shifted, and which rules needed to be explicit for the integration to support the operating model.

## Systems Involved

The landscape included customer management, enterprise operations, ticketing, and middleware. Each system solved a valid need on its own, but the business value depended on how well they behaved together.

## Technical Approach

I paired process mapping with field-level integration analysis, documenting dependencies, business rules, exception paths, and source-of-truth decisions. This made conversations with engineers, vendors, and business stakeholders much more concrete, especially during testing and defect triage.

## Outcome

The engagement improved shared understanding across teams, reduced avoidable rework, and created a more stable foundation for ongoing integration enhancements and operational reporting.
