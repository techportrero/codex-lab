## Business Context

The organization was modernizing a core ERP footprint while several operational teams were still relying on inconsistent processes and undocumented workarounds. Success depended on more than configuration. The business needed a clearer operating model and a shared understanding of how decisions would flow through the system.

## Problem

The project carried classic implementation risk: overlapping stakeholder expectations, uneven process maturity, and limited clarity around how upstream and downstream teams would be affected by new workflows.

## My Role

I owned requirements discovery and helped shape the bridge between business users, implementation partners, and technical teams. That included workshop facilitation, documenting current and future-state flows, identifying decision points, and supporting UAT planning.

## Systems Involved

The implementation touched ERP, finance, procurement, and downstream reporting processes, so requirement quality mattered not only for launch readiness but also for reporting accuracy and operational continuity after go-live.

## Technical Approach

I structured requirements around process outcomes instead of only feature requests, then worked backward into workflow decisions, data dependencies, and handoffs across teams. That framing made it easier to isolate assumptions, define test scenarios, and surface integration implications before they became late-stage surprises.

## Outcome

The result was a stronger line of sight between business intent and configured behavior, with cleaner testing support, fewer unresolved edge cases entering launch, and better operational confidence across participating teams.
