## Business Context

AI tools are increasingly useful for research, drafting, and synthesis, but value depends on whether they can be integrated into real delivery workflows without undermining quality or stakeholder trust.

## Problem

Most AI experimentation stays at the novelty stage. Teams see interesting outputs, but not a repeatable operating model for when to use the tool, how to validate it, and where human review remains essential.

## My Role

I evaluated how AI could support business analysis work in concrete ways, especially around requirement structuring, documentation support, and pulling together signals from multiple inputs more efficiently.

## Systems Involved

The workflow combined large language model tools, internal knowledge assets, and documentation practices rather than acting as a stand-alone chatbot exercise.

## Technical Approach

I treated the workflow as a process design problem: define the inputs, shape the prompts, establish review gates, and make output quality visible enough that people could trust when the tool helped and when it needed correction.

## Outcome

The result was a more practical view of AI adoption, one grounded in repeatable value, quality controls, and better use of analyst time rather than hype.
